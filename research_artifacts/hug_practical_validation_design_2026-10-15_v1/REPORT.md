# Prospective validation of the HUG-to-Shadow pipeline

15 October 2026. This is a scoped evidence inventory and a proposed protocol, not a new mapping acceptance result. This work read saved files and metadata, checked hashes, and tested a record schema. It performed no geometry queries, FK, inference, physics, training, or evaluation generation.

A defensible near-term claim is: **a declared deterministic retargeter correctly realizes its specified Shadow configuration, with measured native-to-Shadow distortion and all refusals disclosed.** This can be checked without asserting that MANO and Shadow have identical surfaces. It also does not require a universal geometric tolerance merely to define the outcome of the executed Shadow attempt. Whether that pipeline supports reliable failure prediction remains an empirical question.

| Claim | Current scope |
|---|---|
| C1. Declared pipeline implementation | Supported by the specific existing decoding, frame, FK, joint-limit, and compiled-scene checks below. A complete frozen pipeline readiness receipt is not issued here. |
| C2. Historical strict native relationship preservation | The nine full-grid failures remain unchanged. |
| C3. Approximate native fidelity within a justified allowance | Optional stronger claim; no justified allowance or independent assessment exists yet. |
| C4. Useful Shadow failure prediction and effects of generator diversity | Not tested; needs later authorized outcomes, locked object splits, and mapper/geometry/coverage controls. |
| C5. Native MANO and Shadow outcome equivalence | Not established and not implicit in C1 or C4. |

The precise current evidence is in `current_claim_manifest.json`, with receipt hashes and limitations. The saved native reconstruction passes for all 32 sources, with zero reported vertex, landmark, and rotation differences against their exports and exact closed-face indices. The original nominal Shadow FK report covers those same 32 hashes and 768 joint-limit checks, with no joint-limit violations. All 32 meet the historical 10 mm **mean** fingertip guard; the maximum mean is 8.305 mm, while the worst individual fingertip discrepancy is 17.518 mm. These are measured native-to-robot errors, not fingertip-to-object contact quality. The legacy wrist-to-palm convention is numerically implemented; that does not establish anatomical identity.

The separate compiled-scene receipt reports 2,756 passing checks on indices 8/12/16/20/24/28, under MuJoCo 3.3.7. It checks the saved geometry representation, transforms and collision configuration. It establishes neither dynamic tracking nor native/robot contact equivalence. Visible object geometry, compiled collision geometry, literal component boundaries, exposed union boundaries, and simulator pairwise contacts must remain separate references. Exposed geometric accessibility is not itself MuJoCo contact output.

The saved proposal inventory has 91 paths and 75 unique pickle hashes. Its well-bound collection has 32 native proposals across four geometries and eight observation groups. All 32 already informed point, frame, anchor, or reference work and are development evidence. Six non-cube cases underwent repeated surface fitting and one cube had a detailed surface investigation. The remaining 25 can broaden **same-object development** checks; they are not untouched grasp or unseen-object validation. The 43 other unique proposal hashes have weaker provenance/exposure histories, so no blind holdout is established from them. Nineteen additional upstream/copied observation files and ten downloaded apple inputs are not additional saved generated proposals; the apple input labels were withheld. `extra_file_disposition.json` records this scoped distinction without deserializing them.

The next implementable steps are:

1. Close the reference-geometry diagnosis on existing evidence, preserving the historical results. Specify which hand/object surface each metric describes, and retain ambiguous or unavailable comparisons.
2. Freeze the continuous measurement contract and manufactured controls before additional measurements. Record individual landmark errors, keyvector differences, same-object distance-field changes, geometric region proxies, signed profiles, conditional normal differences, and representation discrepancy. Do not sum overlapping anatomical areas as physical contact area.
3. Compare a fixed baseline and candidate on the same development inputs, including every refusal and missing metric. Freeze one deterministic mapper version and its input/scene contract. Distortion must be reported, not erased by changing reference semantics or dropping difficult cases.
4. If claiming C3, prospectively justify an error budget from independent requirements or calibration. Keep numerical precision, sampling uncertainty, correspondence ambiguity, and systematic morphology differences separate. No current-case score, paper average, collider margin, or optimizer search bound supplies that budget automatically.
5. For object generalization, acquire an authorized or demonstrably unexposed corpus and lock geometry-level splits across generators before testing. Aliases, transformed copies, decompositions, observations and seeds of one object stay together. Retuning consumes the test set as development evidence.
6. Only after later authorization, test the frozen Shadow pipeline's outcomes and failure predictor. Tie each label to the actual executed configuration and controller, separate mapper refusal from task failure, and control for representation and coverage differences when comparing generator mixtures. A poor native proposal must remain eligible to fail; successful lifting is not a universal mapping criterion.

Existing static data can verify decoding, coordinate transforms, joint mapping/limits, declared pose realization, and bounded geometric discrepancies on its checked domain. It cannot by itself establish unseen-object performance, dynamic tracking, physical task equivalence, or predictive value. C1 can support a clearly scoped later Shadow study; C2–C5 must not be silently promoted by that choice.

The design is operationalized in `protocol_design.json` (seven stages and seven metric families), `case_record.schema.json`, and `decision_logic.json`. Eleven synthetic schema controls pass, including refusing outcome labels without execution, separating mapping refusal from task failure, requiring evidence for C3, and allowing scoped C1 readiness without a C3 budget. These are schema tests, not scientific validation.

Research motivation is limited to metric design: [TopoRetarget](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16272v1) reports contact displacement, bone alignment and penetration separately from tracking; its annotated contacts and concavity exclusions do not transfer to our cases. [AnyTeleop](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.04577v2) supplies a hand-keyvector retargeting precedent, not an object-relative accuracy certificate. `source_motivation.json` binds these concise source summaries and the pinned HUG review; no published averages or thresholds are adopted.

Files under this new directory are the only edits. Prior artifacts and shared project documents were preserved. Read `archive_manifest.json` for the final file closure; authoring scripts are documentary tools, not a new production mapper.
