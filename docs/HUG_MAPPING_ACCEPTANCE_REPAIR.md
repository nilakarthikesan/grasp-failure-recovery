# HUG mapping acceptance: declared Shadow inputs and native fidelity

15 October 2026. This report separates the simulator-input claim needed for the chosen common-robot study from the stronger native-surface equivalence claim. It preserves every historical criterion and result. All work uses existing proposals, static geometry and manufactured controls; no inference, new proposals, physics integration, outcome labels, evaluation dataset or training ran.

## What was wrong

Three different questions had been combined into one signoff: whether we decode HUG correctly; whether MuJoCo realizes our declared Shadow commands correctly; and whether Shadow preserves every tested MANO surface relationship. The first two are implementation questions. The third is a substantially stronger anatomical claim. The user selected failure of the **executed, retargeted Shadow grasp** as the predictor's label.

The old strict checker also could not produce full PASS. Its continuous orientation status is explicitly unresolved, and independent near/far bounds cannot certify equality in a threshold-crossing cell even for identical geometry. All nine saved grids contain native-ambiguous cells. Optimizing until that implementation returned PASS was an invalid stopping rule. Its definite counterexamples remain valid: fixing an incomplete positive certificate does not remove measured errors.

A separate structural-identity certifier now passes 25 manufactured positive/negative controls without changing the historical rule. It proves exact distance-field identity for an explicitly verified labelled geometric identity. Its continuous orientation proof is deliberately limited to outward closed tetrahedra and query triangles with certified facet/normal eligibility. Unsupported geometry remains unproved. This supplies a meaningful positive control; it does not certify MANO and Shadow as identical.

## Actual mapping improvements

The earlier object-aware fit proposed infeasible endpoint steps, so exact selection rejected them. A feasible-step solver now checks the original fingertip caps and exact protected surface/eligibility predicates at each accepted step. A second, separately frozen correction guides the solver using the same closest-point exterior and competing-facet conditions used by the exact checker. It does not soften those conditions.

These mixed normalized values are diagnostics, not physical distances. Across the two stages one orientation row and one proximity row become passing. Every accepted transition retains the original individual fingertip caps, the 10 mm mean guard and protected previously passing/eligible rows. Final mean fingertip errors are 7.139044 mm and 5.322369 mm. The mug's smallest remaining cap slack is only about 8.6 nm; that is a numerical near-boundary result, not a robust physical margin. These are experimental per-case corrections under the historical geometry, not a promoted general mapper. Bounded solver termination does not prove global infeasibility. The original nine full-grid failures are unchanged; retained-row fitting is not a new full-grid certificate.

Sixteen feasible-solver and fourteen eligibility controls pass. Independent saved-output reviews check each accepted transition and the original predicates. Their thousands of arithmetic comparisons are not additional independent grasps.

## Current simulator implementation

The current source-accurate Shadow importer is separately bridged to all 32 saved legacy commands. Independent XML forward kinematics agrees with current compiled task points to 3.991e-16 m. All 32 commands satisfy authoritative source-precision joint limits, without clamping. Current mesh vertex sets agree within 1.020e-8 m, below a prospectively declared 1 micrometre numerical import allowance. This allowance concerns mesh serialization, not anatomical retargeting quality.

The historical importer aliased visual and collision files sharing the same basename. A 6.9446 mm vertex-set discrepancy initially suggested a larger shape error than it demonstrated. A direct numerical comparison of the convex solids gives a maximum distance of **24.512339 micrometres**, not 6.9446 mm; the reverse direction is 19.193627 micrometres. All extreme vertices were checked against the opposite hull. This explains why vertex sampling must not be described as collision-boundary error. The alias is real and repaired in current source, but it does not explain millimetre-scale native-to-Shadow discrepancies. No contact-force or outcome equivalence follows from that static calculation.

The first assembled-scene bridge passes 24 of 32 complete cases: all 32 hand, frame, object geometry, control and source-limit checks pass, but eight milk cases fail the frozen object-inertia limit of 1e-12 kg m². A source-only reproduction identifies the installed MuJoCo eigendecomposition as the cause: 5.8290679768e-12 reconstruction error matches the observed 5.8290679781e-12 within 1.28e-21. A source-tensor-preserving correction prepares an explicit principal frame using a more accurate symmetric eigensolver; original object bytes/content hashes, mass and intended inertia stay unchanged. Twenty targeted static regressions pass, including repeated principal moments and continued rejection of invalid inputs. This is an implementation precision repair, not a new scientific threshold. MuJoCo documents the full-tensor-to-principal-frame conversion in its [3.3.7 XML reference](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/3.3.7/XMLreference.html#body-inertial-fullinertia).

The corrected complete-scene rerun passes **32/32** cases through the current registered `ActuatedShadowRunner`: native camera/wrist-to-palm composition, independent XML root inversion/FK, exact named targets and source limits, hand and object geometry, captured object pose/mass, and declared static control/table settings. Maximum palm position discrepancy is 1.093e-16 m; maximum object-inertia tensor discrepancy is 6.506e-19 kg m². The same numerical gates are used before and after correction. The two runs each use 128 static forwards and zero integration. Saved comparisons confirm every compared non-inertia measurement field is unchanged. These are time-zero command/configuration checks, not tests of physical servo tracking.

A separate current-source check replays the six original native-frame placements and the two saved experimental candidates against 365 retained witness records. The original six retain all 178 definite proximity changes (98 lost, 80 added), with 123 reliable opposite directions, 2 preserved directions and 9 ineligible direction records. The two experimental candidates together have 22 definite proximity changes, 11 unresolved proximity records and 3 preserved records; their directions include 13 reversals and 4 preserved records. Both experimental candidates retain the literal original fingertip caps and 10 mm mean guard on current geometry. Four original poses fail literal comparisons to old fingertip caps by at most 10.172 nm; two of these failures are only about 1e-16 m arithmetic differences. A subsequent pure source-XML readback confirms all 192 individual joint bounds across the eight poses, including both experimental commands, with zero tolerance. These tiny cap differences are recorded without rebasing and are separate from the substantive surface counterexamples. No full-grid or unseen-proposal claim follows from this retained subset. This diagnostic uses 32 static forwards and no new native geometry queries or integration.

## Acceptance statement

**Accepted:** static simulator-input realization of these 32 saved HUG-derived nominal Shadow commands through the hash-bound current native-frame conversion, source-accurate hand/object importer and registered actuated scene. Native proposal identity, named joints and limits, root/palm/world/object frames, collision geometry and declared static controls are verified. This is an engineering signoff for the chosen retargeted-Shadow pipeline on the inspected saved inputs.

**Not accepted:** the stronger claim that Shadow preserves all native MANO surface relationships. Historical strict failures remain failures. The current fixed-witness diagnostic is reported independently above. Neither per-case surface fitting nor the stateless reliability profile is promoted to a generally validated replacement. Fresh generator-to-Shadow solves, dynamic tracking, unseen proposals and predictor generalization are outside this signoff.

This scoped acceptance permits planning a later controlled replay of the declared saved inputs. It does not itself authorize or execute evaluation collection. It also does not turn retargeted Shadow outcomes into native HUG outcomes: the scientific comparison concerns generator-plus-retargeter pipelines on a common robot.

This is not a native HUG outcome benchmark. The proposed study must retain the generator, native proposal, retargeter profile, original and final Shadow command, object/hand geometry and execution protocol with each future outcome. A failure label belongs to the actual recorded Shadow execution. Bad native proposals must remain available; mapping rejection, generator failure and completed physical failure must not be conflated. Multiple-generator comparisons need to disclose retargeting as part of each proposal-to-execution pipeline.

The historical legacy mapper remains the default. A separate stateless reliability profile fixes reproducibility/loss-gradient defects but was not geometrically accepted. The two new surface-fit candidates are not a general replacement. The present saved-command acceptance must not silently extend to fresh retargeting, modified assets, new profiles, unseen objects or dynamically reached configurations.

## Why these criteria are justified

HUG's own work reports MANO simulation and embodiment-specific robotic retargeting; it does not prescribe our zero-changed 1/2/5 mm anatomical surface rule or a Shadow-specific error tolerance. Our fingertip-to-native-target discrepancy is also different from HUG's fingertip-to-object metric. [HUG paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17054v1), [official implementation](https://github.com/KevinyWu/hug).

Numerical frame/joint/geometry checks justify a claim about the implemented Shadow configuration. The unchanged strict surface tests assess the stronger native preservation claim and still expose differences. Neither claim alone validates a predictor. Independent downstream prediction evidence remains necessary once simulation collection is explicitly authorized.

## Evidence and reproduction

The complete source, prospective protocols, controls, selected candidates, static measurements, independent reviews and source/runtime hashes are archived at `research_artifacts/hug_mapping_acceptance_repair_2026-10-15_v1/`. Its archive manifest maps immutable files back to their original local paths; runtime asset files are hash-bound and not copied; derived diagnostic arrays are included. Large JSON is losslessly compressed with both original and archive hashes. Each protocol version retains its own scientific meaning. Archived scripts retain original absolute paths to avoid silently changing their source hashes; use their recorded runtime and bindings when reproducing them.

Runtime preparation failures and warnings are retained. The first hand-bridge launch refused a NumPy-version mismatch before model construction; a second lacked Pydantic. The successful current bridge used NumPy 2.2.6, MuJoCo 3.3.7 and Pydantic 2.13.5. Eleven manufactured hand controls were repeated successfully in that declared runtime during the bridge, not before its initial authorization. Saved-value independent arithmetic checks avoid the matrix operation that emitted warnings; no unverified cause for those warnings is claimed. These numerical checks are not rigorous outward-rounded certificates of physical behaviour.
