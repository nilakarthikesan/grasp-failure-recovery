\begin{abstract}
Grasp-failure predictors trained on simulated attempts may encounter unfamiliar
objects and configurations whose coverage depends on the grasp proposal source.
We investigate whether training with proposals from multiple generators improves
generalization relative to single-generator training, using HUG, ContactGen,
DexGrasp Anything and UniDexGrasp as candidate sources for a common Shadow Hand
task in MuJoCo. Before collecting comparison data, we audit each source's native
representation, coordinate conversion, articulation and object-relative surface
geometry, separating mapping fidelity from eventual task success.
Completed HUG development checks support numerical reconstruction and kinematic
composition, but reject strict surface preservation across six detailed cases
and three finer repeats under a project-defined equivalence criterion, distinct
from HUG's generator benchmark. Practical adequacy for the executed Shadow
task requires separately justified distortion limits and independent evidence.
Constrained finger fitting, per-grasp palm
placement and direct surface-constraint fitting have not established a
validated replacement. Historical HUG prediction diagnostics also
show that useful failure ranking can coexist with warnings on successful
unfamiliar attempts. The multiple-generator training comparison and independent
generalization evaluation remain unexecuted, so improved prediction is a
hypothesis rather than a reported result.
\end{abstract}

% BEGIN GFP SECTION introduction
\section{Introduction}
Simulation makes it practical to collect labeled grasp attempts for training an
outcome predictor. The resulting training distribution depends on which grasps
are proposed, how those proposals are translated to the simulated hand, and how
the hand executes them. A predictor exposed to several proposal sources might
encounter a broader range of grasp configurations than one trained from a single
source. Broader coverage need not improve prediction: additional sources can
introduce redundant examples or different translation errors.

Our central research question is:
\begin{quote}
Does training a grasp-failure predictor on simulated grasps from multiple
generators improve generalization to unfamiliar objects and grasp configurations
compared with single-generator training?
\end{quote}
We propose comparisons using one, two, three and four proposal sources under a
common hand, task, execution protocol and predictor specification. Matched
selected training counts distinguish source diversity from supplying more
examples; they are an experimental control rather than the research question.
Object-held-out and generator-held-out tests address distinct generalization
claims. These comparisons have not yet been executed.

HUG, ContactGen, DexGrasp Anything and UniDexGrasp supply different native
representations and generation stages \cite{hug,contactgen,dga,unidex}.
Their published grasp-quality results do not certify our translations.
We audit each proposal against its own declared native configuration before
interpreting simulator outcomes. Mapping validation supports the prediction
study: it neither requires every proposal to succeed nor guarantees downstream
generalization. The target is eventual grasp, lift and hold completion from a
closing prefix, rather than slip onset or a certified stable-grip decision.

\subsection{Related work}
Failure prediction and recovery are established topics. Gilday et al. combine
sensor-based grasp prediction with wrist-driven recovery and discuss errors on
novel objects using different successful grasp modes \cite{gilday}. FIPER
explicitly distinguishes benign unfamiliar observations from failures in
generative robot policies \cite{fiper}. SAFE studies multitask failure detection
\cite{safe}; SAFECAST investigates contrast-set training and calibration under
visual and language shifts, and reports that adding more perturbations does
not uniformly help \cite{safecast}. These results motivate controlled diversity
comparisons, not a claim that nuisance warnings or augmentation are new ideas.
Our proposed contribution is a controlled study of multi-source simulation
coverage for failure prediction, supported by explicit embodiment-specific
mapping audits. No new algorithm or real-world transfer result is claimed.

Pick-success models already test targeted data coverage: Amazon's Robin study
compares broader pick locations with an equal-sized collection
\cite{robin}. Vision and touch also support after-closing, before-lift outcome
prediction across diverse objects \cite{calandra}. These precedents motivate
checking measurements, calibration and baseline comparisons before attributing
our errors to wrist coverage. The proposed training comparison remains under
scope review; wrist-feature contributions alone do not demonstrate its remedy.

Recent proprioceptive work also learns a hand-local grasp-readiness score
alongside reactive control \cite{blindreflex}. We therefore do not claim that
command-relative features or pre-lift monitoring are new; our proposed
contribution concerns source-diversity and representation comparisons in a
common simulation task.

\section{Grasp sources and simulator mapping}
\subsection{Validation framework}
Each source record identifies its input geometry, physical scale, coordinate
frames, hand assets, checkpoint, configuration and output stage. We first
reconstruct the native proposal and independently check transformation,
joint-order and forward-kinematic consistency. We then compare native and
translated configurations in a shared object frame, reporting per-finger
landmark errors, object proximity, penetration, lost and added near-surface
regions, and reliable surface-direction comparisons. Visual and collision
surfaces are distinct references, with unmatched regions and sampling uncertainty
retained in every report.

Numerical decoding, geometric preservation and execution adequacy are separate
claims. Agreement of joints or fingertips does not establish equivalent
collision surfaces, contact forces or task outcomes. Poor native proposals
remain in the audit; successful lifting is not a mapping acceptance criterion.
Thresholds and sampling assumptions are declared explicitly, and inspected
development cases are distinguished from later independent validation.
Our common executor differs from source-specific policies and quality tests.

For the current HUG development audit, a frozen strict encoded-surface claim
is refuted by a positive same-part lost/added proximity-area lower bound or a
reliable object-facing sign reversal, under declared numerical assumptions.
An absent sampled reversal cannot certify continuous normal agreement. That
claim defines strict preservation, not a universal tolerance for all useful
retargeters. An approximate simulator-pipeline claim would require separately
justified distortion budgets and independent adequacy evidence; it cannot
retroactively turn the recorded HUG failures into passes.

Provider audits and their evidence-backed manuscript subsections can proceed
in parallel. Shared retargeters, assets, measurement code and execution settings
are versioned dependencies: a change requires reviewing affected audits.
The current phase uses static reconstruction of saved inputs and manufactured
checks. It generates no new evaluation episodes, physical outcome labels or
training data. Actual-native validation remains pending where authentic source
outputs or assets have not been established.

\section{Historical HUG execution and predictor reference}
The following protocol and fitted predictor describe the completed HUG-only
development study. They motivate the broader question but are not mixed-source
training results. Controller versions and their original labels remain distinct;
the common prospective protocol must be declared before new collection.
\paragraph{Grasp source and simulation.}
HUG supplies grasp hypotheses from rendered RGB-D observations \cite{hug}.
Dex Retargeting maps the proposed human hand pose to an articulated right
Shadow Hand \cite{dex}; MuJoCo supplies physics \cite{mujoco}. We use HUG
proposals and selected development assets, not official benchmark grasp labels
or a HUG-Bench evaluation. A fixed sequence approaches, closes, settles, lifts
and holds through force-limited position servos. Physics steps are 2 ms;
recorded control intervals are 40 ms. We train an outcome predictor, not the
grasp controller. Initial RGB-D is saved; there is no online image stream among
the predictor inputs.

\paragraph{What noise means.}
The original \path{fixed_grasp_lift_v2} protocol adds an episode-constant
bias to 22 finger targets during designated closing and subsequent phases.
For a fixed draw $\boldsymbol\xi\sim\mathcal U([-1,1]^{22})$ and bound
$a\in\{0,0.15,0.60\}$ radians,
\[
 \mathbf u_t=\operatorname{clip}_{[\mathbf l,\mathbf h]}
 (\mathbf u_t^{\rm nominal}+a\boldsymbol\xi).
\]
The two wrist biases are zero. Requested offsets, clipped targets and measured
motion are distinct saved fields. This varies commands, not object identity,
obstacles, observation noise or a validated hardware error distribution.
Nonzero bounds share paired draws within a proposal and repetition. Noise can
improve or harm the grasp; the executed outcome determines its label.

\paragraph{Success and failure.}
A completed attempt has $y=1$ when the frozen scorer records qualifying contact,
peak lift at least 0.14 m, at least 2 s of consecutive HOLD samples above the
initial object center plus 0.12 m, final height loss at most 0.02 m, no approach
table collision and no timeout. Otherwise a completed task has $y=0$.
Setup errors and runtime clearance stops have null labels and remain outside
task-outcome metrics. Labels describe task completion, not physical slip onset;
threshold-adjacent acquisitions and category precedence are audited separately.

\paragraph{Causal inputs and fitting.}
Twenty closing samples from 2.20 through 2.96 s precede the first LIFT sample
at 3.00 s. Each contains 24 joint positions, 24 velocities and 24 applied targets.
Last value, mean, population standard deviation and last-minus-first change
produce 288 statistics; 269 are active under training-only normalization.
L2 logistic regression predicts success, with
$p_{\rm failure}=1-p_{\rm success}$ and an alert at $p_{\rm failure}\geq0.5$.
Validation log loss selects $\lambda=0.1$ from
$\{0.001,0.01,0.1,1\}$. Commands-only and training-prevalence baselines use
consistent splits. Outcomes, contacts, object truth, identities, injected noise
metadata and future samples are excluded from inputs. An alert is a model
prediction, not a physical failure or a controller intervention.

\section{Historical HUG development evidence}
\paragraph{Original pilot.}
Sixteen proposals in four observation families from a cube and a HUG apple
produce 1,040 attempts: 444 successes, 596 failures and no runtime errors.
Training, validation and test contain 520, 260 and 260 whole trajectories,
respectively, grouped by observation family. Both geometries occur in training;
the test is one reserved apple observation family. Splits were selected after
collection and inspection, so results are retrospective development evidence.
Failures comprise 590 acquisitions, two missing-contact tasks and four
scorer-defined drops, insufficient for a slip forecasting benchmark.

On this test, state plus command features attain failure AUROC 0.8523, versus
0.6419 for commands alone; log loss is 0.3771 and Brier score 0.1170.
At the fixed threshold the full model detects 187/202 failures, misses 15,
and falsely warns on 22/58 successes. The commands-only model warns on
192/202 failures and 52/58 successes. Better ranking than this ablation does
not establish reliable deployment or calibrated physical probabilities.

\paragraph{Fresh evaluation and executor correction.}
With model, normalization, threshold and v2 execution frozen, 2,080 new attempts
produce 659 successes and 1,421 failures. AUROC is 0.9173 on fresh cube/apple
scenes and 0.5588 on pooled new milk-carton/mug attempts. Geometry, mass and
failure composition change together; this is not an isolated object-shape
comparison. All 520 mugs collide during approach before the prediction cutoff,
so mug false alarms on successes cannot be estimated from this batch.

The Shadow forearm does not fit the original mug poses. Version
\path{fixed_grasp_lift_v3_table_clearance} changes wrist configuration and
compensates the carrier transform to preserve intended world palm and finger
geometry. Its nominal table clearance is 10 mm; a runtime guard checks an
actual 1 mm margin every physics step. This is a controller correction, not
prediction-triggered recovery. Checks planned before scoring contain 24 mugs
and three controls. Twenty-six finish without table contact; one high-noise

\paragraph{Static feasibility constraint.}

continuous wrist range. A comparison using these stored training pairs cannot
honestly contrast familiar and unfamiliar safe mug wrist commands.

\section{Planned multiple-generator comparison}
\paragraph{Status and validity gate.}
The source-diversity study remains proposed. Native mapping claims and their
limits must be documented before collecting comparison labels. The current
HUG strict-preservation failures and other providers' missing actual-native
evidence cannot be presented as completed four-source validation. A declared
approximate-pipeline study needs its own justified adequacy checks.

\paragraph{Compare source count and source identity.}
Compare predictors trained from one, two, three and four proposal generators.
With four sources there are 15 nonempty source subsets: four single-source,
six two-source, four three-source and one four-source condition. Evaluating
only one nested source chain would confound source count with generator identity.
Whether to execute all subsets or preregister a feasible balanced subset remains
an experimental-design choice, not a completed experiment. Matched selected
training counts control for added data; source diversity does not itself imply
better coverage or improved prediction.

Keep the canonical hand, physical object pool, task/executor and labels,
predictor inputs/architecture and tuning budget consistent. Declare source
quotas, class-mixture handling and grouping before collection; fit normalization
on training only. Retain inference cost, generated/selected/rejected counts and
mapping refusal reasons. An adapter rejection is not a physical task-failure
label. The generic provider recorder still needs the closing velocity and
applied-command fields needed by the historical predictor; input compatibility
must be established before using that predictor as a reference.

\paragraph{Generalization and baselines.}
Use one independent object-held-out evaluation pool across conditions; keep
related observation/proposal families and their disturbances in the same split.
Held-out-generator evaluation is a separate transfer question. Tune operating
points and calibration on validation only, freeze models before test scoring,
and report false warnings on successful tasks together with missed failures,
ranking, calibration and uncertainty respecting shared families. Undefined
failure recall when a group has no failures remains explicit.

Use the same predictor specification for the primary source-diversity ablation.
Architecture comparisons and compatible external predictors answer additional
questions; published scores on different tasks/hands/test sets are not direct
baselines. The original logistic predictor and its 0.5 threshold remain a
historical reference, not evidence for the new comparison. Neither always-warning
nor always-clearing behavior meets both objectives. No source mixture, new
predictor, calibration or fresh holdout has been fitted or scored in this phase.

\section{Limitations and evidence availability}
The four-source training/generalization comparison remains unexecuted and
actual-native mapping validation is incomplete for three sources. HUG
retargeting preserves neither all proximity relationships nor all reliable
directions under the declared strict development claim. These limitations
cannot be hidden by numerical/FK passage or successful task outcomes.
Our historical predictor diagnostic concerns one hand, fixed task definitions and reused
mug cases under a corrected executor. Simulation contact fidelity, real-world
transfer, usable warning lead time and prediction-driven recovery are untested.
The task target is eventual grasp, lift and hold completion, not impending
physical slip. Prior literature already studies nuisance warnings and diversity;
a distinct scientific contribution still requires a convincing controlled
comparison or informative negative finding. Static feasibility constrains that
comparison and should not be replaced by unsafe or mismatched trials.
